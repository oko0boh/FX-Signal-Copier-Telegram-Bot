## Summary
SMT divergence, seasonal tendencies and quarterly shifts are all confluences,
never standalone ideas. SMT compares correlated markets at a key level and key
time. Seasonals and quarterly shifts only shape the higher-timeframe narrative.

## Definitions
- **SMT divergence** [S, clear], p.172–173: a discrepancy between correlated
  instruments. Symmetric: the dollar index makes a lower low and the foreign
  currency makes a higher high, which confirms the flow. Non-symmetric: the
  foreign currency fails to make the higher high, which warns of a reversal.
- **Pairs** [S, clear], p.174: EURUSD with GBPUSD, EURUSD with DXY, USDJPY
  with USDCHF, AUDUSD with NZDUSD; BTC with ETH; gold with DXY; gold with
  silver; ES with NQ. p.180 uses DXY and EURUSD.
- **Seasonal tendency** [S, clear], p.168: a roadmap of past performance over
  15–40 years; a confluence only. Charts of 20–35 years for CAD, AUD, EUR are
  shown (p.169–170).
- **Quarterly shift** [S, clear], p.188–189: about every 3–4 months the market
  changes profile or order flow. The 3–4 months are counted from the most
  recent structural change, not from calendar quarters.

## Rules / conditions
- [ ] Only SMT divergences at HTF key levels, CRT highs/lows, key times,
  previous day/week highs/lows, key structure or key profiles count (p.174).
- [ ] The best SMT forms at key times and in the direction of the HTF draw
  (p.178). Never use SMT as the main idea (p.178). Trading every SMT leads to
  losing (p.174).
- [ ] Romeo Q&A (handwritten): the answer to a missed entry is SMT, using
  related assets as the benchmark (p.68, partly).
- [ ] A consolidation quarter is followed by expansion; a bearish quarter
  followed by a bullish shift last month points to a bullish quarter (p.190).
- [ ] The strongest quarterly shifts come with a monthly/weekly SMT (p.194).
- [ ] An example stack that points bullish (p.195): last 3–4 months bearish
  with expansion, a major SMT, next months seasonally bullish, a discount key
  level tested.
