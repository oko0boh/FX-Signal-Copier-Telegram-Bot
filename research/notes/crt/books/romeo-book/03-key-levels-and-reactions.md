## Summary
A key level is a higher-timeframe point where price is expected to expand or
reverse. The book lists the types and the three ways price reacts, and gives a
table that pairs a CRT timeframe with the timeframe its key level comes from.
It does not give a ranking algorithm for choosing between several levels.

## Definitions
- **Key level (S)** [clear], p.83, 205: an important price point or zone on a
  higher timeframe where large expansions and reversals happen. Types listed:
  higher-timeframe highs and lows (daily, weekly, monthly), FVG, breaker block,
  order block, rejection block, "all other HTF PD arrays".
- **Key level by structure (R)** [clear], p.4–5, 45: the important highs and
  lows are the **low that broke a high** and the **high that broke a low**
  (a break of structure). The condition for a key level is that the high or
  low created the BOS (p.5 handwritten).
- **Order block (S)** [clear], p.83, 107: a candle (or run of candles) that
  either takes liquidity (sweeps a low or high) or fills liquidity (fills an
  FVG or retests an order block), and is then closed through. Zone rule,
  p.109–111: if price closes only just beyond the open, use the body; if it
  closes beyond the candle's high/low, use the whole candle; if the wick is
  clearly larger than the body, use the wick.
- **Breaker (S)** [clear], p.84: the high that took out a low (or the low that
  took out a high), when retested.
- **FVG (S)** [clear], p.84: a gap between consecutive candles; a turtle soup
  on a lower timeframe can form inside it.
- **Old lows of the month/week/day** [R, clear], p.11: previous lows of
  months, weeks and days are strong places. A six-month low is drawn as an
  example.

## Rules / conditions
- [ ] A key level only matters if you can find an entry model there (p.205).
- [ ] It must come from a higher timeframe than the one you enter on (p.205).
- [ ] The three reactions (p.39–40, 206):
  V shape (explosive, one tap), M/W shape (most common; first tap builds a
  lower-timeframe liquidity pool, second tap reverses), triple MMM/WWW (worst
  for a turtle soup trader; level hit several times).
- [ ] A double touch of a level on a low timeframe is a single touch on the
  higher timeframe (p.69 handwritten).
- [ ] A timeframe stays bullish/bearish only until it reaches a key level of
  the next timeframe up; a 5m high/low counts only until a 15m level appears,
  and so on (p.61).
- [ ] Expect a deep entry inside the PD array before price reflects (p.62,
  partly).
- [ ] Order of importance when judging HTF bias: highs, lows, FVGs, opens,
  closes (p.60, clear).
- [ ] A failed order block is a strong pattern; it expands to the high of the
  failed block's swing (p.63, partly). The best breaker is the one with the
  higher-timeframe bias (p.25).
- [ ] An older rule from the same family: any concept that has no turtle soup
  inside it is a loser (p.51, opinion).
- [ ] Key level + market profile gives an outcome; see
  `06-market-profiles-structure-and-orderflow.md` (table, p.87).

## Timeframe for the key level (S, p.205, clear)
Key level for a CRT is one step above it (the slide writes "+ by 4"):
15m CRT = 1h (+ 4h) level; 1h = 4h (+ daily); 4h = daily (+ weekly); daily CRT
= weekly (+ monthly); weekly CRT = monthly (+ yearly).

## Entry, stop, target
- Entry: at the key level once a turtle soup forms there (p.27, 40).
- Stop: the swing high (or low) that formed into the key level (p.27
  handwritten, clear).
- Target: the opposing key level or FVG; the target of the day is the DOL
  (p.5 handwritten: "where is the magnet zone, your entry, the opposing end
  of your exit").

## Conflicts with other sources
- The community guide in rulebook 6.2 says exactly five key levels (OHP, OLP,
  FVG, OB, rejection block). This book adds breaker blocks and "all HTF PD
  arrays" (p.205) and keeps highs/lows. It is consistent with the ICT-extras
  variant.
