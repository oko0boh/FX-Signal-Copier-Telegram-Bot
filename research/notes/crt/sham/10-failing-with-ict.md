## Summary
Sham says traders fail with ICT because they study it like a school subject:
too many concepts, too many key levels, too many entry models. They also lack
structure: no top-down hierarchy, no trade-framing framework, nothing that ties
the pieces together. His fix has five steps: strip everything down, give each
concept one job, give each timeframe one job, standardise every trade with one
checklist, and repeat. No chart examples. (Promo for a September 3rd Telegram
project.)

## Definitions
- **Allowed key levels (simplified set)**: **order blocks, fair value gaps and
  significant highs/lows**. Sham says these are enough to build his framework.
  *(7:44)*
- **Timeframe hierarchy**: each TF gets one job. HTF narrative → directional
  idea → directional bias → context or dealing range (intermediate TF) → trade
  framing (LTF) → execution. *(10:25, 13:34–13:50)*

## Rules / conditions
- [ ] Use only one mechanical, objective entry model. *(8:15–8:50)*
- [ ] Limit key levels to OB, FVG and significant swing highs/lows. *(7:44)*
- [ ] Do not enter just because price tests a key level and an LTF entry model
  appears at a key time. That gives repeated stop-outs before the real
  expansion. A trade-framing step (context on the intermediate TF) is needed
  first. *(11:28–12:15)*
- [ ] Every trade goes through the same checklist, conditions and confirmation
  standards. *(16:28–16:44)*
- [ ] Drop any concept that does not help with bias, context, timing, framing or
  execution. *(15:08–15:24)*
