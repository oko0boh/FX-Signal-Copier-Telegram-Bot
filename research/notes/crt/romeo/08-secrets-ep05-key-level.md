## Summary
This episode defines a key level and lists the topics around it (marking, types, reactions, timing).
Only two are covered: how LTF price behaves at a HTF key level, and the trade opportunities
it offers. The key claim: on the way down to a bullish key level, price prints failing
bullish CRTs. Then it prints a convincing fake bottom with a fake MSS just above the level, followed by a
final sweep into the level (kiss of death TS). After that comes a true MSS and expansion. You can trade both
the move into the level and the move away from it. The example is an Ethereum trade (Bitcoin also shown,
with ETH as SMT). The first third is promo (the "one-year rule", the $1M bounty, the "what do you see" posts).

## Definitions
- **Key level**: a price point at which you expect price to give a bounce. The
  bounce is either a counter-trend reaction or the actual high or low of the candle. *(8:18–8:53)*
- Key levels can be **price** levels or **time** levels. *(5:46–6:03)*
- Key levels are "usually a higher time frame key level". You study LTF behaviour at
  the HTF level. *(9:41–9:57)*
- **Fake bottom / fake MSS (FMS)**: a convincing bottoming pattern, sometimes with a market
  structure shift, formed just *before* (above) a bullish key level. Price then sweeps down into
  the level = **kiss of death turtle soup**. *(13:19–13:51, 15:13)*
- **True MSS**: low, then high, then lower low, then price breaks through "the high that broke the low".
  Here it comes from the key level at the correct time. *(13:51–14:06, 16:46–17:03)*
- Three skill stages: (1) cannot mark levels, (2) can mark but not trade them, (3) can mark and trade. *(7:10–9:25)*

## Rules / conditions
- [ ] Bullish case: do not buy bullish CRTs that form on the way down toward a bullish
      HTF key level. They are expected to fail. *(12:13–12:46, 13:04)*
- [ ] The first bottoming pattern / MSS that forms *before* price touches the key level is
      assumed fake. Romeo claims "always ... no exception". *(14:57–15:13)*
- [ ] A valid long needs: price trades into the key level (the KoD sweep of the fake bottom), then a
      true MSS forms from the key level. *(13:36–14:06, 17:19–17:34)*
- [ ] Confluence on the shown trade: OTE retracement after the MSS plus SMT (BTC vs ETH). *(17:03)*
- [ ] Two trade opportunities: (a) sell the journey down into the key level, (b) buy from
      the key level after the true MSS. Romeo trades both. *(15:38–16:46)*
- [ ] Trading BTC requires watching ETH, and the reverse (SMT pair). *(10:45)*

## Entry, stop, target
- Entry (long from level): after the true MSS out of the key level. A pullback to the OTE is shown. *(16:46–17:03)*
- Entry (short into level): sells on the way down. No exact trigger given. *(16:30)*
- Stop: not stated.
- Target: not stated for the long. For the short, the key level itself.
- Timeframe pairs used: HTF key level, LTF entry. Specific TFs not named.
- Time-of-day / session filters: "correct time" is mentioned but not defined. *(13:51–14:06)*

## Vague / discretionary parts
- **How to mark the key level**: not taught in this video (promised later). Candidate
  measurable sources from other episodes: HTF candle open of a down-close candle (ep.6),
  previous candle high/low, an HTF FVG edge.
- **"Bounce"**: define it as a reversal of at least X x ATR(LTF) or at least 50% of the prior leg.
- **"Very convincing pattern before the key level"**: define it as any LTF bullish CRT or MSS whose
  low is above the key level, within distance D (e.g. less than 0.5 x HTF ATR).

## Conflicts with other sources
- In ep.2 the KoD is the last sweep before the *target*. Here it is the last sweep into the *key level*
  before the reversal. These are consistent if you treat a key level as a target: the KoD is the
  final liquidity grab before any target / level.
